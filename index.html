About

What I Build

My main interests sit at the intersection of statistical decision-making, machine learning, and systems. I like building tools that take messy, real data and turn it into something reusable, interpretable, and useful for making decisions.

This includes experimentation, production data pipelines, reusable schemas, unstructured text extraction, explainable modeling, and deployment-oriented ML workflows.

Research Thread

I am also interested in AI research at the intersection of knowledge distillation and mechanistic interpretability. To transfer knowledge effectively from one model to another, I first need to understand what knowledge is represented inside the model, how it is being used, and which signals are most useful to pass on.

How I Think

My background is unconventional. Before moving into data science, I studied philosophy at UBC, which shaped how I reason under uncertainty, communicate ambiguous findings, and think critically about what a model or experiment is actually claiming.

I also bring experience in psychology, neuroscience, and teaching, which helps me think about how people perceive information, make decisions, and learn complex ideas.

This combination is what draws me to work where rigor and communication both matter: designing experiments, building reliable ML systems, interpreting model behavior, and helping teams turn evidence into better decisions.
